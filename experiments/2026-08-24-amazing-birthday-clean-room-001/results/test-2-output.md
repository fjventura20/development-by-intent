# Amazing Birthday — February 29, 1960

**Monday, February 29, 1960** was already unusual before anything happened: it was Leap Day, a date that would disappear from the calendar for the next four years.

But this particular February 29 was anything but a quiet extra day.



You were born into a world poised between eras: the Cold War was intense, the American civil-rights movement was accelerating, spaceflight was still experimental, television was reshaping popular culture—and, late that very night, a Moroccan city would suffer one of the most devastating earthquakes of the 20th century.

## The defining event of your exact birthday: Agadir

At **11:40 p.m. UTC on February 29**, a shallow earthquake struck almost directly beneath **Agadir, Morocco**.

Its magnitude was only about 5.9—normally not a number associated with enormous catastrophe—but its location and vulnerable buildings made it devastating. The U.S. Geological Survey estimates that roughly **12,000–15,000 people were killed and about 25,000 injured**. Some districts were virtually destroyed. ([USGS Earthquake Hazards](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/iscgem878424/impact?utm_source=chatgpt.com "M 5.9 - 2 km WNW of Agadir, Morocco"))

The earthquake lasted less than 15 seconds.

That contrast is what makes the event so striking: a moderate earthquake, a tiny geographical area, and enormous human consequences.

So while families elsewhere were welcoming babies born on the rarest date on the calendar, Agadir was experiencing a catastrophe that would permanently reshape the city.

## Something much gentler also began that day: *The Family Circus*

On **February 29, 1960**, cartoonist Bil Keane's *The Family Circus* made its newspaper debut.

That is an exact-date connection, not surrounding context. King Features confirms that the first cartoon ran that day. ([King Features Syndicate](https://kingfeatures.com/2011/11/bil-keane-creator-of-the-family-circus-dead-at-89/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "BIL KEANE, CREATOR OF THE FAMILY CIRCUS, DEAD AT 89 – King Features Syndicate"))

What makes this connection especially fitting is longevity.

A comic strip born on the same day as you would go on to become a fixture of American newspaper culture for generations. The humor was deliberately ordinary—children, parents, misunderstandings and family life—precisely the sort of everyday experience that continued while the larger world around it changed dramatically.

You and *The Family Circus* therefore started your journeys on exactly the same Monday.

## Chicago opened a very different cultural institution

That same day, the **first Playboy Club opened in Chicago**, at 116 East Walton Street.

Contemporary historical accounts and even later federal court records identify **February 29, 1960** as its opening date. ([Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/2017/8/19/18453576/playboy-swings-puts-spotlight-on-hugh-hefner-and-the-business-of-music?utm_source=chatgpt.com "'Playboy Swings' puts spotlight on Hugh Hefner and the business of music - Chicago Sun-Times"))

Whatever one thinks of Playboy, the club was culturally significant. It represented a changing America—nightlife, celebrity culture, jazz, shifting sexual attitudes and increasingly aggressive lifestyle marketing.

The coincidence becomes even more interesting because performers associated with that first club included figures from a rapidly changing entertainment world.

So February 29, 1960 produced two remarkably different pieces of American popular culture: one centered on suburban family life, the other on sophisticated adult nightlife.

That is quite a range for one birthday.

## You have an exact birthday twin: Tony Robbins

Another child born on **February 29, 1960** was **Tony Robbins**, later one of America's best-known motivational speakers and self-help authors. ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/economics-magazines/robbins-anthony-j?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Robbins, Anthony J. | Encyclopedia.com"))

This is more than sharing February 29.

You share the **actual date and year**.

Both of you entered the world on the same Leap Day, and both have now lived 66 chronological years even though February 29 itself has returned only **16 times** since 1960.

That is one of the peculiar pleasures of being a leapling: the calendar makes your chronological age and your count of literal birthday dates look wonderfully incompatible.

## Your birthday also belongs to one of opera's giants

February 29 has another distinguished occupant.

**Gioachino Rossini**, composer of *The Barber of Seville* and *William Tell*, was born on **February 29, 1792**. The Museo Nazionale Rossini confirms the date. ([Museo Nazionale Rossini](https://museonazionalerossini.it/eng/biography/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Biography - Museo Nazionale Rossini"))

That means that when you were born, it was the **168th anniversary of Rossini's birth**.

There is something appropriate about a Leap Day producing a composer famous for extraordinary speed: Rossini wrote dozens of operas, often under astonishingly tight deadlines.

Your calendar date doesn't appear often, but when it does, it has attracted some interesting company.

## The civil-rights movement was accelerating around you

Now we move from exact-date events to the **world immediately surrounding your birth**.

Just **28 days before you were born**, on February 1, four Black college students sat down at the segregated Woolworth lunch counter in Greensboro, North Carolina, asked to be served and refused to leave when service was denied.

Their protest helped ignite a wave of student sit-ins across the South. ([Smithsonian Institution](https://www.si.edu/object/greensboro-lunch-counter%3Anmah_1425301?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Greensboro Lunch Counter, 1960 | Smithsonian Institution"))

This distinction matters: **the Greensboro sit-in did not begin on your birthday.** It was already underway when you arrived.

But it tells us something important about the America you entered.

Legal racial segregation remained a reality. The Civil Rights Act was still four years away. The Voting Rights Act was five years away.

Someone born in February 1960 could therefore witness within childhood the dismantling of a legal system that had existed for generations.

## And 32 days after you were born, humans began watching Earth's weather from space

On **April 1, 1960**, just over a month after your birth, NASA launched **TIROS-1**, the world's first successful experimental weather satellite.

Its purpose was wonderfully basic by today's standards: determine whether television cameras aboard a satellite could photograph Earth's cloud cover.

They could.

TIROS-1 operated for only 78 days, but it demonstrated that satellites could revolutionize weather observation. ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/tiros/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "TIROS - NASA Science"))

Look at the lifetime arc hidden inside that fact.

When you were born, **satellite weather observation was an experiment that had not yet worked**.

Today, satellites continuously monitor hurricanes, wildfires, atmospheric temperatures, oceans and global climate while forecasts appear instantly on a device in your pocket.

You did not merely live through improvements in weather forecasting.

You were alive **before space-based weather forecasting really began**.

## Your lifetime crosses an extraordinary technological divide

That TIROS connection opens the larger story.

You were born before any human being had traveled into space. Yuri Gagarin would make the first orbital flight the following year.

You were three when Kennedy was assassinated.

Nine when humans first walked on the Moon.

In your twenties when the personal-computer revolution accelerated.

In your thirties when the World Wide Web entered ordinary life.

In your forties as Google and broadband transformed access to information.

In your fifties as smartphones made permanent connectivity normal.

And in your sixties as conversational artificial intelligence began allowing ordinary people to communicate with machines using natural language.

In 1960, a computer capable of serious calculation might occupy a room.

In 2026, you can hold a conversation with an AI system capable of researching the circumstances of **the exact Monday on which you were born**.

That is a substantial technological journey for one lifetime.

## The special character of February 29, 1960

There are birthdays with more famous events attached to them.

But February 29, 1960 has something different: **contrast**.

On your exact day, Agadir experienced terrible destruction.

A beloved family comic began appearing in newspapers.

A nightclub that would become an emblem of 1960s popular culture opened its doors.

Tony Robbins was born.

And the date itself carried the anniversary of one of opera's great composers.

Meanwhile, immediately around you, young Americans were challenging segregation and engineers were preparing humanity's first practical look at Earth's weather from orbit.

You entered a world that was still recognizably the postwar world of the 1950s—Cold War rivalry, segregation, landline telephones, newspapers and broadcast television.

**Sixty-six years later, almost every one of those organizing assumptions has changed.**

And there is one final numerical curiosity that captures your unusual starting point rather nicely:

You have lived through **66 years of history**.

But the date printed on your birth certificate has appeared on the calendar only **16 times since the day you were born**.

For a person born on February 29, even the calendar makes an ordinary lifetime look extraordinary.
